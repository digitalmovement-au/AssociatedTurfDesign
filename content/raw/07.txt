## Questions About Installing a New Lawn
What does a professional lawn installation include?
A good installation covers choosing the right variety, preparing the soil, levelling the surface, laying the turf with tight staggered joins, rolling it for good soil contact and giving it a deep first watering. We will confirm exactly what your quote includes and what preparation your site needs before installation day, so there are no surprises.
Do I need to remove my old lawn first?
Yes. Old grass and weeds should be removed so they do not grow back through your new turf. Smaller areas can be cleared with hand tools, while larger areas may need a turf cutter or herbicide. Dispose of the old lawn properly and then improve and level the soil. Water the area lightly a day or two before installation so it is moist but not muddy. Avoid fertiliser that is not watered in properly, as it can burn the new turf.
Can turf be installed on clay soil?
Yes, but clay needs some help first. Heavy clay drains poorly and can become hard and compacted, so it is worth breaking it up and blending in quality topsoil or a sandy loam. Good drainage helps prevent waterlogging and root rot and gives your new turf a much better start. Our team can advise how much soil improvement your site is likely to need. Aim for around 100 to 150mm of quality soil so the root system can establish properly.
What is the best time of year to install turf?
Spring and autumn are ideal in Sydney, with warm soil and milder temperatures. Summer installation works well with diligent watering, and winter is also a good option thanks to lower evaporation and less weed pressure, although top growth slows until spring. We install turf all year round and will let you know if a particular variety is better suited to your timing. In winter, new turf needs less water to establish but should never be allowed to dry out.
What are the signs of a poorly installed lawn?
Watch for gaps or overlaps between rolls, low spots where water pools, uneven growth and weeds coming through the new turf. Most of these come back to rushed preparation or tired turf, which is why a careful turf installation with fresh grass makes such a difference.
How do I choose the right installer?
Ask where the turf comes from, how fresh it will be on the day and what preparation is included. Check their reviews, ask how long they have been in the industry, find out how they handle aftercare questions and make sure the quote clearly explains what is and is not included. An installer that grows its own turf can answer all of these with confidence.

## Questions About Having Turf Laid
Do you lay turf for homes as well as large projects?
Yes. We supply and lay turf for backyards, front lawns and new homes, as well as for landscapers, builders, schools, councils and sporting grounds. For large open areas with good access, we can also lay Maxi Turf rolls by machine for a faster, neater result with fewer joins.
How should the ground be prepared before laying?
Remove old grass, weeds and debris, then improve the soil with quality topsoil or a sandy loam if it is heavy clay or very sandy. Rake the area level with a gentle fall away from buildings so water drains properly. The Associated Turf team will talk you through what your site needs before laying day, and we are always happy to answer questions if something on site does not look right.
How long after laying can I walk on the new lawn?
Keep foot traffic to a minimum for the first few weeks while the roots take hold, and avoid walking on areas you have just watered. Most new lawns are ready for light use once they are firmly rooted and have had their first mow, usually around three to four weeks after laying. Mow with sharp blades and avoid cutting too low for the first few mows. Most lawns thrive at around 30 to 40mm.
How often should new turf be watered after laying?
Give the turf a deep soak straight after it is laid, then water daily for the first two to three weeks so the soil underneath stays moist. Early morning is the best time. Watering between 5am and 9am reduces evaporation and gives the grass time to dry before nightfall. After that, gradually reduce watering to deeper, less frequent soakings to encourage strong, drought-tolerant roots. Never let newly laid turf dry out completely, especially on hot or windy days.
Can turf be laid in winter?
Yes. Sydney's mild winters suit turf laying, as cooler weather means less evaporation and less weed pressure. Top growth slows, but the roots keep developing, so the lawn is ready to take off when the weather warms in spring. New turf still needs regular watering, just less often than in summer. In frost-prone or heavily shaded spots, avoid laying late in winter.
What is involved in laying Maxi Turf rolls?
Maxi Turf rolls are 1.2 metres wide and 12 metres long and are laid by our laying machine. They suit open areas with easy access such as parks, ovals, golf courses and acreage. The bigger rolls mean fewer joins, quicker establishment and less water to get the turf settled. Ask our team whether your site is suitable for Maxi rolls.

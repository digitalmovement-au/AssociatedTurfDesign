## Getting Your Site Ready for Commercial Turf Installation
The quality of the finished lawn is set long before the first roll goes down, as skipping preparation can result in uneven growth and weed invasion. The area should be cleared of debris and weeds, and the soil improved with quality topsoil or a sandy loam where the existing ground is heavy clay or very sandy, aiming for around 100 to 150mm of quality soil. Levels should be smooth and graded to drain away from buildings and paths, so water does not pool on the new lawn after heavy rain.
Irrigation should be installed and tested, or water available on site, because new turf needs a deep soak immediately after installation and daily watering for the first two to three weeks. If you are unsure whether the site is ready, our team can talk through what is needed before we lock in a date. A little checking up front avoids costly rework later.

## Questions About Project Turf Installation
What types of commercial projects do you install turf for?
Associated Turf installs turf for residential developments, display homes, schools, councils, parks, streetscapes, sporting grounds and commercial landscapes such as corporate and hotel grounds. Whether the area is a few hundred square metres or a large open space, we can recommend the right variety and installation method for each part of the site.
Should we use standard rolls or Maxi Turf?
Maxi Turf suits large, open areas with easy machine access, such as ovals, parks, golf courses and acreage. Smaller or more complex areas with garden beds, paths and tight corners are usually better suited to standard rolls. Our team can assess your site and recommend the best option, and some projects use both.
Who prepares the site before installation?
Site preparation is usually handled by the builder or landscaper as part of the earthworks and soil works. We will explain exactly what the soil, levels and drainage need to look like and confirm the site is ready before installation day, so the turf goes down onto a good base. If your site needs extra soil works, let us know when you request a quote.
How long before an installed lawn can be used?
Keep traffic off new turf for the first few weeks while the roots establish. Most lawns are ready for light use once they are firmly rooted and have had their first mow, usually three to four weeks after installation. Our team can advise on a sensible timeline for your site, based on the variety and how the area will be used. Kikuyu and couch establish fastest, while buffalo varieties are slower to establish but lower maintenance once settled.
Can you install turf in winter?
Yes. Sydney's mild winters are a good time for commercial turf installation, with less evaporation and weed pressure. Growth slows, but roots continue to develop, so the lawn is well placed to take off in spring. Avoid laying late in winter in frost-prone or heavily shaded areas, and keep up regular watering even though less is needed than in summer.
What care does the turf need after handover?
Water deeply and regularly while the turf establishes, ideally in the early morning, then switch to deeper, less frequent watering once the roots have taken hold. Mow with sharp blades and avoid cutting too low, and fertilise three to four times a year with a slow-release fertiliser. We provide clear care advice for your site or facility team, so the lawn keeps performing long after the project is complete.

## Questions About Laying Large Areas
How big are Maxi Turf rolls?
Each Maxi Turf roll is 1.2 metres wide and 12 metres long. They are much larger than standard rolls, which is why they are laid by machine rather than by hand. The bigger rolls cover large areas quickly and leave far fewer joins in the finished surface, which is especially valuable on sporting fields.
What kinds of sites suit machine laying?
Machine laying suits large, open areas with easy access, such as sports ovals, parks, golf courses, school grounds, communal open spaces and acreage. Sites with lots of garden beds, paths, trees or tight corners are usually better suited to standard rolls, or a mix of both. Send us a site plan and our team will recommend the best approach.
Why do fewer joins matter?
Every join is an edge that has to knit together while the turf establishes, and a large field laid with standard rolls has a great many of them. Fewer joins mean a smoother, more uniform surface that knits together faster. On sporting fields and parks, that helps the area look better and become usable sooner. It also reduces the amount of hand finishing needed after laying.
How should the site be prepared for laying?
The area should be cleared of weeds and debris, the soil improved with quality topsoil or a sandy loam where needed, and the surface levelled with good drainage. Irrigation should also be installed and tested. Water the soil lightly a day or two before laying so it is moist but not wet. Our team will confirm what your site needs beforehand, so the machine and crew can work efficiently once they arrive.
How soon can a newly laid field be used?
It depends on the variety, the season, the weather after laying and how the area will be used. Turf needs time to root firmly before it takes heavy traffic, so keep use to a minimum for the first few weeks while it establishes. Mowing should wait until the turf is firmly rooted, usually three to four weeks after laying. We will give you clear advice on watering, mowing and when light and full use can begin, so you can plan bookings and events with confidence.
Can you lay standard rolls on commercial sites too?
Yes. The Associated Turf laying service covers standard rolls as well as Maxi Turf, so we can handle edges, garden surrounds and smaller areas around the main open space. We can also deliver standard rolls on pallets if your own crew prefers to lay them, or combine both approaches on the same project.

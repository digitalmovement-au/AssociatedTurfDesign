## Lawn Preparation Sydney
A lush, healthy lawn starts with proper preparation. Before laying new turf, the ground must be carefully cleared, levelled, and nourished to give your grass the best chance to thrive. In Sydney’s variable climate, good lawn preparation is the key to long-term success.
#### Steps for Preparing Your Lawn
**1. Clear the Area**
- Remove existing grass, weeds, and debris.
- Use a turf cutter, herbicide, or hand tools for smaller areas.
- Dispose of old lawn matter so it doesn’t regrow.
**2. Improve the Soil**
Sydney soils can vary from sandy to clay-heavy, so soil conditioning is essential:
- Add organic matter or compost for nutrients.
- Mix in quality topsoil or sandy loam to improve drainage.
- Use a rotary hoe to blend soil conditioners evenly.
**3. Level the Ground**
- Rake and level the area for a smooth surface.
- Fill in low spots to prevent water pooling.
- Ensure a gentle slope away from buildings for proper drainage.
**4. Fertilise the Base**
- Apply a starter fertiliser or soil improver before laying turf.
- Water lightly to settle the soil and activate nutrients.
**5. Lay Turf Promptly**
Once prepared, turf should be laid the same day it arrives. Roll it out in a brick pattern, pressing edges tightly together.
**6. Water Immediately**
Give the turf a deep soak straight after installation, then water daily for the first 2–3 weeks to help roots establish.
#### Why Lawn Preparation Matters in Sydney
- **Hot summers**: Proper soil preparation helps retain moisture and protects turf from stress.
- **Heavy rain**: Good drainage prevents waterlogging and root rot.
- **Soil types**: Sydney’s mix of sandy and clay soils means amendments are often needed for long-term turf health.
#### Best Lawns for Sydney Conditions
- **Sir Walter Buffalo**: Shade tolerant and low-maintenance.
- **Matilda Soft Leaf Buffalo**: Soft underfoot, child- and pet-friendly.
- **Kikuyu**: Fast-growing, ideal for high-traffic areas.
- **Couch (Greenlees Park & Wintergreen)**: Excellent for sunny spots and sports areas.

## Matching the Right Turf to Your Yard
It is tempting to choose a lawn by looks alone, but conditions matter more. Sunlight is the first thing to check. Soft-leaf buffalo varieties such as Matilda and Sir Walter perform with around three to four hours of direct sun a day, while Kikuyu and the couch grasses need full sun, ideally six hours or more, to stay thick and healthy.
Next, think about how the lawn will be used. Kids, dogs and backyard cricket call for a hard-wearing variety, while a front lawn might be all about a fine, manicured look. Kikuyu and Greenlees Park Couch handle heavy wear in full sun, while Matilda offers very good wear tolerance in partial shade. Finally, consider how much time you want to spend on maintenance each week. Buffalo grows more slowly than Kikuyu and couch, so it needs less mowing and fertilising. Kikuyu, on the other hand, recovers quickly from wear but spreads into garden beds if the edges are not kept trimmed. Getting these choices right at the start saves time, water and money for years to come.

## How to Lay Your Turf Once It Arrives
The best results from turf supply and delivery come when the turf goes down quickly, so have the soil prepared, levelled and lightly moist before the truck arrives. Start laying along a straight edge such as a driveway or path, and roll the turf out like a rug. Butt every join tightly without overlaps or gaps, and stagger the joins in each new row like brickwork. Use a large, sharp knife to shape around trees, garden beds and borders.
On hot days, water each section as you go rather than waiting until the end. Once the last roll is down, give the whole lawn a deep soak, then water daily for the first two to three weeks. Rolling the lawn helps smooth out small bumps and air pockets, although the turf will settle on its own over time. Keep off the lawn as much as possible while it roots, and avoid walking on areas you have just watered.

## Questions About Ordering Delivered Turf
What do I need to have ready before my turf arrives?
Have your soil prepared, levelled and lightly watered, and make sure there is clear access for the delivery truck. Plan to lay the turf the same day. If your site has steep driveways, narrow access or restrictions, let us know when you book.
Should I water the soil before my turf arrives?
Yes. Water the soil lightly a day or two before laying, so it is moist but not wet. This helps settle the soil and gives the turf a moist bed to establish its roots. Avoid making the soil too soggy, and if you are laying in hot weather, start early in the morning or late in the afternoon so the turf does not dry out too quickly.
What tools do I need to lay turf?
A large, sharp knife for shaping around trees, garden beds and borders, a rake for levelling and a hose or sprinkler for watering the turf in. A lawn roller is useful for smoothing out small bumps and air pockets, but it is not essential because the turf will settle naturally. A timer or smart controller on your sprinkler helps avoid overwatering and ensures even coverage.
What if I cannot lay all the turf on the day it arrives?
Turf is best laid on the day it is delivered. Turf remains a living product after harvesting, and prompt laying helps minimise stress and gives the grass the best opportunity to establish. Fresh turf roots faster and stays greener than turf that has been left waiting. If you know in advance that you cannot lay on a certain day, call us and we will move your harvest and delivery to suit.
Can my landscaper order delivered turf directly from you?
Yes. Landscapers and builders have ordered straight from the Associated Turf farm for over 45 years. We can deliver to the site on the day they plan to lay, and large orders can be loaded on pallets. If they would rather not lay it themselves, our team can also lay it for them, which some customers prefer for larger jobs.
When can I mow my newly laid lawn?
Wait until the turf is firmly rooted, usually around three to four weeks after laying. Avoid mowing until the grass is around 50 to 70mm tall. Use sharp blades and only take the tips off for the first few mows. Cutting too low too soon puts new turf under stress. Most lawns thrive at around 30 to 40mm once established.

Why choose supply and installation instead of buying turf on its own?
Laying it yourself saves on labour, but it is hard physical work and the result depends on good preparation and technique. Is installing turf myself cheaper?
On paper, yes, because you are not paying for labour. For small, flat areas many homeowners are happy to do it themselves. For larger, sloping or awkward areas, professional installation often gives a better and faster finish, and mistakes such as gaps or poor soil contact can cost more to fix later. We are happy to quote both so you can compare.
What do I need to do before installation day?
Remove old grass, weeds and debris, then improve the soil with quality topsoil or a sandy loam if it is heavy clay or very sandy. Rake the area level with a gentle fall away from the house. Make sure there is water available and clear access for our team. Water the soil lightly a day or two beforehand so it is moist but not muddy. We will confirm what your site needs when we quote, so there are no surprises on the day.
Can my landscaper use your package?
Absolutely. Landscapers and builders can order straight from our farm, or hand the turfing over to us entirely. Our package can be scheduled around their timeline, so the lawn goes down once the rest of the landscaping is complete.
What happens if it rains on installation day?
Light rain is usually no problem and can even help the new turf settle into the soil. Heavy rain can make the soil too soft and muddy to work properly, so if the forecast looks bad we will talk to you about whether to go ahead or move the day. Because we harvest to order, early notice makes rescheduling much easier.
How do I look after the lawn once it is installed?
Water daily for the first two to three weeks, preferably in the early morning, and keep traffic to a minimum while it roots. After that, switch to deeper, less frequent watering and fertilise three to four times a year with a slow-release fertiliser.
